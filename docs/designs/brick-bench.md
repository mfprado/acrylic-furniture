# Brick bench

A bench whose seat is London stock bricks laid flat on Ø8 mm chrome rods, on a T-slot aluminium profile frame built like the Profile desk. Idea: #4.

## Rules

- Bricks lie flat with the 228 mm side front to back, touching, no gap.
- Seat length is bricks along the length × 108 mm: 864 mm by default, 324 to 1512 mm.
- Seat depth is rows × 228 mm: 228 mm by default, up to 684 mm.
- Two rods under each brick, one near each side of the brick width, running front to back.
- Each rod is held at both ends by a rod support block bolted with T-slot bolts into the middle slot of the inner face of the long rails, so the rod axis is at mid profile height.
- The rails go round the bricks: the brick area is exactly the seat (bricks along the length × 108 by rows × 228), and the frame is one profile wider on every side. The top of the rails stands half a profile above the rod axis, so it surrounds the lower part of the bricks. The seat height is measured to the top of the bricks.
- The profile starts on 2020. With profile on auto, the site picks the lightest profile (20, 30 or 40) whose long rails stay within the sag limit (span / 300) under the chosen load.
- The frame has legs, long and short top rails, low stretchers and levelling feet, as the desk.

## Warnings

- The long rails sag under the load (default 100 kg), checked like the desk's rail sag check. The default 2020 profile at 8 bricks shows this warning and suggests 3030.

## Assembly

1. Cut the profile: 4 legs, 2 long rails, 2 short rails, and the low stretchers.
2. Short and long rails: bolt corner gussets under each end, T-nuts loose.
3. Legs: slide onto the gussets, top flush with the rails. Two U frames.
4. Join the U frames with the short rails. Tighten.
5. Low stretchers and back corner gussets, same as the desk.
6. Screw a levelling foot into each leg.
7. Rod blocks: drop T-nuts into the middle slot of the inner faces of both long rails. Bolt two blocks per brick position, one on the front rail and one on the back rail, one at each end of every rod.
8. Slide an Ø8 rod into each facing pair of blocks, front to back. Tighten the clamp screws.
9. Lay the bricks on the rods, inside the rails, two rods under each brick, touching.
10. Level with the feet, then re-tighten every bolt once.

## Look in 3D

Red-brown bricks, each a slightly different shade, with a recessed top and LONDON BRICK on it like the reference picture; chrome rods; grey frame like the desk. A switch hides the bricks to show the frame and rods.

## Unknown, to measure

- Product listing for the rod support block, and its height, width, mounting hole size and bolt size.
- Rod length: taken as the seat depth, between the inner faces of the long rails; blocks may shorten it.
- Whether a 32 mm hole spacing fits the 20, 30 and 40 profile slots.
- Exact brick size and weight of the bricks used; the bench uses 68 mm height, the designer's range was 68 to 73 mm.
- Whether the bench keeps the low stretchers.
- Whether the rod blocks can mount mid profile on the inner face (block width and hole size).
